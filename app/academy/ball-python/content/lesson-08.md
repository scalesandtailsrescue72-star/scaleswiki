# Feeding

Proper nutrition is one of the foundations of good Ball Python husbandry. A healthy feeding routine provides the nutrients and energy your snake needs while reducing stress and minimizing feeding-related risks.

Most captive Ball Pythons thrive on appropriately sized frozen/thawed rodents offered on a regular schedule.

---

## Choosing the Right Prey

Prey should be appropriately sized for your Ball Python.

A general guideline is selecting prey that is approximately the same width as the widest part of the snake's body.

Oversized prey may increase the risk of regurgitation or digestive problems.

---

## Frozen/Thawed vs. Live Feeding

Frozen/thawed rodents are generally recommended because they:

- Reduce the risk of injury
- Are easier to store
- Provide complete nutrition
- Are considered the safer option for most captive snakes

If frozen prey is used, it should be thawed completely and warmed appropriately before feeding.

---

## Feeding Schedule

Feeding frequency varies depending on:

- Age
- Size
- Body condition
- Individual metabolism

Young Ball Pythons generally eat more frequently than adults.

---

## After Feeding

Allow your Ball Python time to digest its meal.

Avoid unnecessary handling for approximately 48 hours after feeding whenever possible.

Maintaining proper temperatures is especially important for healthy digestion.

---

## Feeding Response

Not every Ball Python eats on a predictable schedule.

Temporary food refusals can occur because of:

- Seasonal changes
- Shedding
- Husbandry issues
- Stress

Always review enclosure conditions before assuming illness.

---

## 🐍 Did You Know?

Ball Pythons have heat-sensitive pits around their mouths that help them detect warm-bodied prey, even in very low light.

---

## 💡 Keeper Tip

Keeping a feeding log helps you monitor feeding dates, prey size, sheds, and changes in appetite over time.

---

## Key Points

- Feed appropriately sized prey.
- Frozen/thawed rodents are generally recommended.
- Avoid handling after feeding.
- Maintain proper temperatures for digestion.
- Record feeding activity to monitor long-term health.
