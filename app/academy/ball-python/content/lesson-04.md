# Preparing Before You Buy

One of the biggest mistakes new keepers make is purchasing a Ball Python before they are fully prepared.

Responsible reptile ownership begins long before you bring your snake home. By taking the time to prepare your enclosure, equipment, and care plan first, you'll reduce stress for both you and your new Ball Python.

## Build the Enclosure First

Your enclosure should be completely assembled before your Ball Python arrives.

This includes:

- Appropriate enclosure
- Two secure hides
- Water bowl
- Suitable substrate
- Heating equipment
- Thermostat
- Thermometers
- Hygrometer
- Decorations and climbing opportunities

Never assume you'll have time to finish the enclosure after bringing your snake home.

---

## Test Everything

Once the enclosure is built, allow it to run for several days before introducing your Ball Python.

During this time, verify that:

- Warm side temperatures remain stable.
- Cool side temperatures remain appropriate.
- Humidity stays within the recommended range.
- All heating equipment operates safely.
- Your thermostat is functioning correctly.

Making adjustments now is much easier than after your snake has arrived.

---

## Choose a Healthy Animal

Whether purchasing from a breeder or adopting through a rescue, look for a Ball Python that appears alert and healthy.

Avoid snakes showing signs of illness such as:

- Wheezing
- Bubbles around the nose or mouth
- Visible mites
- Retained shed
- Significant weight loss
- Obvious injuries

When possible, ask about the snake's feeding history and previous care.

---

## Find a Reptile Veterinarian

Do not wait until your Ball Python becomes sick to search for veterinary care.

Locate an experienced reptile veterinarian before bringing your snake home.

Having an established veterinary relationship can save valuable time during an emergency.

---

## Budget for Long-Term Care

The purchase price is only one part of ownership.

Plan for ongoing expenses such as:

- Food
- Replacement substrate
- Electricity for heating
- Veterinary examinations
- Equipment replacement
- Emergency medical care

Responsible ownership means planning for both expected and unexpected expenses.

---

## If You Already Own Reptiles

New reptiles should be quarantined before introducing them into a collection.

A proper quarantine period helps reduce the risk of spreading infectious diseases or parasites to your existing animals.

Always use separate equipment during quarantine and wash your hands between handling animals.

---

## Before Bringing Your Ball Python Home

Make sure you can answer **yes** to each of these questions:

- Is my enclosure fully assembled?
- Have I tested temperatures and humidity?
- Do I have food ready?
- Do I know where my reptile veterinarian is located?
- Can I commit to caring for this snake for decades?

If the answer to any of these questions is **no**, take the time to prepare before bringing your Ball Python home.

---

## 🐍 Did You Know?

Many husbandry problems begin before a snake ever enters its enclosure. Careful preparation is one of the best ways to prevent stress and health issues.

---

## 💡 Keeper Tip

Never rush into buying a reptile.

A few extra days spent preparing can prevent months—or even years—of avoidable husbandry problems.

---

## Key Points

- Build and test the enclosure before purchase.
- Buy quality equipment from the beginning.
- Locate a reptile veterinarian before you need one.
- Budget for long-term care.
- Preparation is one of the most important parts of responsible ownership.
